About

Mechanisms first. Predictions that travel.

I am an Associate Research Scientist in Earth and Environmental Engineering at Columbia University. I study how ecosystems, water, climate, and energy systems respond to environmental extremes, integrating field and flux-tower observations, satellite remote sensing, process-based models, and physics-guided and interpretable AI.

Featured work

From scientific gaps to reusable insight

Three examples show how I combine physical reasoning, environmental data, and machine learning to answer consequential Earth-system questions.

01

Evapotranspiration
GRL · 2019

Physics-constrained machine learning

Problem: Flexible machine-learning estimates can violate the physical structure of land-surface exchange.

Method: Embed physical constraints in a data-driven evapotranspiration model.

Impact: An ESI Highly Cited Paper, placing it in the top 1% of Geosciences papers.

Read paper ↗
02

Ecosystem memory
HESS · 2026

Learning evaporative fraction with memory

Problem: Snapshot models miss how antecedent conditions shape surface-energy partitioning.

Method: Learn evaporative fraction while explicitly representing environmental memory.

Impact: Accepted and in production at Hydrology and Earth System Sciences; selected as a Highlight Paper; the linked EGUsphere preprint had 4,521 PDF downloads as of 16 August 2026.

View preprint ↗
03

Climate & power
CEE · 2023

Extreme heat and fossil-fuel reliance

Problem: How do temperature extremes alter power generation and progress toward decarbonization?

Method: Analyze temperature extremes alongside power-generation and emissions observations.

Impact: Revealed increased fossil-fuel reliance during extreme-temperature events across the continental United States; Top 94th percentile among all research outputs tracked by Altmetric.
